Methodology

A traceable path from promise to evidence to acceptance.

1into1 does not treat “project complete” as proof. We reconstruct the acceptance chain and show where evidence supports the milestone, where it is partial, and where it is missing.

Core model

The implementation evidence chain.

Contract / SOW→Deliverable→Acceptance criterion→UAT / Test→Evidence→Defect status→Acceptance→Payment
Review steps

How a milestone is assessed.

1

Define the milestone

Identify the contractual scope, requested amount and the acceptance event being reviewed.

2

Normalize acceptance criteria

Turn clauses, scope statements and deliverables into explicit conditions that can be checked.

3

Map the evidence

Connect UAT, test results, migration evidence, sign-offs, defects and handover records to those conditions.

4

Classify the gap

Distinguish supported, partially supported and unverified items, with the reason visible.

5

Connect to value

Where the project provides a defensible allocation, connect evidence status to the milestone value at risk of premature acceptance.

6

Explain the next question

Produce concrete questions for the implementation partner rather than a generic risk score.

Evidence status

Supported

The supplied evidence materially supports the relevant acceptance condition.

Partial

Some evidence exists, but the chain is incomplete, conditional or affected by unresolved defects.

Unverified

The evidence provided does not substantiate the acceptance condition.

What we do not do

No invented certainty.

  • We do not infer completion simply because a status report says “done”
  • We do not invent evidence that was not provided
  • We do not convert legal ambiguity into a legal conclusion
  • We do not instruct the buyer to pay or withhold payment
Quality boundary

Every conclusion should be explainable.

If a finding cannot be traced back to the supplied project material, it should not appear as a factual conclusion. Where the evidence is incomplete or ambiguous, the review says so.
