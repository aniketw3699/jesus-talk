Prayer for anxiety and fear

When anxiety narrows your attention to everything that could go wrong, prayer can help you slow down, name the fear honestly, and return to Scripture without pretending the situation is easy.

Pray about this nowOpen the Bible

Where to begin

Begin by naming the fear rather than fighting it: what are you afraid will happen, what is within your control, and what is not? A prayer can hold all three. Philippians 4:6–7, Psalm 56, John 14, and 1 Peter 5 are useful passages to read in context when worry is loud.

Prayer is not a demand that you stop feeling anxious. It can be a way to bring fear into the open, ask for steadiness, and choose the next wise action. If anxiety is severe, persistent, or affecting safety or daily functioning, spiritual support can sit alongside qualified mental-health care rather than replace it.

Keep the next step simple

You do not need to solve the whole situation in one prayer. Name what is true, bring the specific burden to God, read the passage in context, and choose one faithful next step you can take today.
