Prayer for grief and loss

Grief does not move on a neat schedule. These guides make room for lament, memory, anger, numbness, questions, and hope without rushing you toward a quick spiritual answer.

Pray about this nowOpen the Bible

Where to begin

The Bible does not treat grief as spiritual failure. The Psalms contain lament, Jesus weeps in John 11, and many biblical prayers speak from confusion before they reach any sense of resolution. That gives you permission to pray from where you actually are.

You can begin with one sentence: “God, this is what hurts today.” From there, remember the person or loss, ask for strength for the next hour rather than the next year, and read a short passage slowly. Grief can also need community, pastoral support, and professional care; prayer does not have to carry the entire burden alone.

Keep the next step simple

You do not need to solve the whole situation in one prayer. Name what is true, bring the specific burden to God, read the passage in context, and choose one faithful next step you can take today.
