# Category-Creation Strategy

Last updated: 2026-09-29

## Strategic shift

We are no longer primarily searching for an existing software category with a pricing gap.

The new method is:

1. Start from large, recurring global pain.
2. Verify that the pain is real using current user complaints, research, and successful narrow products.
3. Ask whether the pain is currently fragmented across multiple point solutions.
4. Design a new horizontal product/category that solves the underlying problem across tools.
5. Require a believable willingness-to-pay path.
6. Prefer local/device-first architecture so free usage costs the developer almost nothing.
7. Do not confuse "new category" with "nobody has ever experimented with any adjacent feature." Adjacent products are acceptable; the requirement is that the proposed product category and value proposition are materially broader/different.
8. Do not build until a kill-test proves demand, differentiation, technical feasibility, and monetisation.

## New evaluation questions

For every category-creation hypothesis, test:

- Is the pain experienced by millions of users or by valuable professional users?
- Is the pain frequent enough to create pull?
- Do people already pay meaningful money for partial/vertical solutions?
- Are current solutions fragmented, vertical, or app-specific?
- Can one coherent product unify the problem in a way incumbents do not?
- Can the product be tested by a normal computer user without specialist industry knowledge?
- Can most processing happen locally?
- Is the free version cheap for us to operate?
- Can the product become a platform/standard, not just a feature?
- Is there a credible moat from accumulated compatibility, provenance data structures, integrations, or workflow adoption?
- Could a large incumbent copy the idea quickly?
- What is the narrow wedge that proves value before attempting the full category?

## Core lesson

The aim is not novelty for novelty's sake.

A good category-creation opportunity should look like:

**large existing pain + demonstrated willingness to pay for partial solutions + fragmented incumbents + a new unifying product model.**
