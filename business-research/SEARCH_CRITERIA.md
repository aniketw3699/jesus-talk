# Search Criteria

Last updated: 2026-09-29

## Strong positive signals

- Customers already spend meaningful money in the category.
- Pain is urgent, frequent, expensive, or operationally important.
- Core processing can happen locally.
- Free usage costs us almost nothing.
- No genuinely comparable free product gives users 80–90% of the paid value.
- Existing competitors are expensive, fragmented, outdated, difficult, locked to hardware/ecosystems, or missing important workflows.
- We can match the proven core and add several important research-backed capabilities.
- Differentiation is strategically important, not just a prettier UI.
- SEO/free tools can attract qualified users.
- Product can sell globally.
- There is a credible path to become #1 in a clearly defined subcategory or a major player.

## Strong rejection signals

Reject when one or more of these dominate:
- a mature free/open-source product already replaces most of the paid workflow;
- the only differentiation is lower price or prettier UI;
- the moat requires years of integrations, proprietary data, hardware support, or regulation;
- success depends on large cloud/GPU/storage spend before revenue;
- workflow is service-heavy or requires human operations;
- category leaders are already inexpensive and mature;
- acquisition depends almost entirely on expensive ads/sales;
- market is too small or too jurisdiction-specific;
- compatibility/support burden is disproportionate to revenue potential;
- exact proposed gap is already being attacked by several credible startups.

## Required final decision for every deep test

- **GO** — strong enough to proceed to product specification.
- **MODIFY** — market is viable but positioning/product must change.
- **REJECT** — do not build and do not repeatedly research.
